Let’s get Mira moving
Choose a topic. You can leave this guide open while you follow the steps.

Connect one of three ways

Bluetooth

  1. Insert the robot’s batteries and turn it on.
  2. Allow Bluetooth or Nearby devices access if Mira asks.
  3. Open the connection badge above and choose Connect beside the robot.

Bluetooth talks directly to powered robots without a cable or wireless controller.

USB cable

  1. Remove the robot’s batteries.
  2. Connect its small control board to the laptop with a USB-C data cable.
  3. Wait for the badge above to show the robot.

The computer powers one robot in this desktop-only mode.

Radio

  1. Insert the robot’s batteries.
  2. Look for a red motor-board light and a slowly blinking blue control-board light.
  3. Connect the ESP32-C3 Mini Pro remote board to the laptop by USB-C.

If prompted, set the new board up as a Wireless controller. This desktop mode can reach several robots over ESP-NOW.

No computer? Use batteries or a USB-C phone charger, then press the small BOOST button to alternate between Home and built-in moves.

Make a safe first move

  1. Choose one robot under Robots Detected.
  2. Click Home. The arm should point straight up with its claw closed.
  3. Keep Joint Mode selected.
  4. Move one slider slowly. Each track labels the physical direction: Base turns left/right, Shoulder and Elbow move down/up, and Claw moves open/closed.
  5. When Joint Mode feels familiar, try XYZ Mode. Click or drag inside a green area in the Top, Side, or Front view to move immediately. You can also enter coordinates and click Move robot. Red targets explain why a position is not reachable.
Keep fingers, hair, and clothing clear. Click the red Stop all button if anything looks wrong.

Teach Mira a sequence

  1. Move the robot into a pose.
  2. Click Capture Pose.
  3. Move it again and capture the next pose. Repeat.
  4. Click Play to preview the sequence.

Make Home straight

Hand-built robots and small servos differ slightly. Calibration saves a small correction so software Home matches the real straight-up pose.

  1. Select one robot, then click Home and Calibrate.
  2. Adjust Base, Shoulder, and Elbow until the arm faces forward and stands vertically.
  3. Adjust Grip, then use Test open and Test closed to check both claw endpoints.
  4. Click Apply Calibration, then Home to check it.
Still far from straight? If a slider reaches its limit, calibration cannot fix the assembly. Home the powered robot, then have an adult remove only the crooked joint’s servo horn, hold the arm section straight without turning the servo shaft, and refit the horn on the nearest tooth. Reinstall its screw, then calibrate the small remaining difference.

Try Mira’s ready-made moves

  1. Select one robot—or intentionally select All Robots.
  2. Click a move to start it.
  3. Click the highlighted move again, or click Stop all, to stop.
